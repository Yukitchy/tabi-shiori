OPTION 1

A garden on the bay, then the fish market

Hamarikyu is a 17th-century shogun's garden sitting between the skyscrapers and the water, with tidal ponds that rise and fall with Tokyo Bay. Paths are flat and there are benches the whole way. Then a short taxi ride to Tsukiji for food.

  • 3300-year-old pineOne tree near the entrance was planted in 1709 and is held up by a forest of wooden props. It is the thing people photograph.
  • 4Tsukiji outer market afterwardsTen minutes by taxi. This is the old market's shopping street — the wholesale part moved to Toyosu, but the food stalls and knife shops stayed. Most shops open on Monday and Tuesday. We eat sitting down, not standing in the street.

OPTION 2

The national collection, indoors and seated

The Tokyo National Museum in Ueno holds the largest collection of Japanese art in the world — swords, armour, screens, Buddhist sculpture, 12th-century scrolls. It is the single best indoor day in Tokyo if walking is the problem, because there are benches in almost every room.

OPTION 3

One small museum, then the view

The Suntory Museum of Art sits inside Tokyo Midtown in Roppongi. It is deliberately small — lacquer, glass, textiles, ceramics, one floor, quiet. Then a short taxi to Tokyo Tower and the main deck, 150 metres up, with seats and a cafe at the window.

OPTION 4

Inside the castle, where the city started

The East Gardens are the old inner keep of Edo Castle — the stone foundation of the main tower is still there, and you can stand on it. It is free, it is in the middle of Tokyo, and almost nobody hurries. Wide flat lawns, benches, one gentle slope.

  • 2The tower that burned in 1657The keep of Edo Castle stood here and was never rebuilt after the fire. The stone base survives. It is a short ramp up and the view over the lawn is the reward.
  • 3The guard housesTwo original wooden buildings from the shogun's time are still standing inside the gate, and you can go in.
  • 4Rest house halfwayThere is a covered rest house with seats and toilets roughly in the middle of the route, so the walk is split in two.
  • 5Afterwards, MarunouchiFive minutes by taxi to the brick station building and a long unhurried coffee. Or straight back to the hotel if you have had enough.

OPTION 5

Dinner on a boat, sitting still for two hours

A yakatabune is a low wooden-roofed boat with tatami seating and a kitchen on board. You board, you sit, dinner is served course by course, and the boat moves down the Sumida and out into Tokyo Bay and back. About two and a half hours in which you do not stand up unless you want to.
